## Product decisions

- AxoBoard focuses on customization, celebrations, ease of use, My Sounds, and branded team competition.
- Launch pricing hypothesis: Starter $99/$79 annual equivalent, Growth $249/$199, Scale $599/$499, and Enterprise from $1,500; unlimited dashboards and viewers on all paid plans.
- Durable $5K MRR target: 5 Starter, 16 Growth, and 3 Scale customers at annual-equivalent pricing = $5,076 MRR.
- Leucistic pink and warm white remain dominant; aquatic blue supports navigation/depth, while GFP green is reserved for live, success, momentum, and rare glow moments.
- Murphy Kombat is a configurable preset, not a hardcoded AxoBoard feature identity.
- Customer assets and branding remain tenant-scoped and versioned.
- Scoring events must be deterministic, idempotent, traceable, and replay-safe.
- Celebration controls require quiet hours, rate limits, mute controls, and reduced-motion support.

## Known limits

- Prototype simulates audio and publish actions; no backend exists yet.
- Sprite and sound uploads are UI concepts without storage, scanning, or licensing workflows.
- Google Fonts are loaded remotely by the prototype and need self-hosting for production reliability.
- Remote beta sessions are held in memory and must be re-created automatically after a dashboard service restart.
- Google now uses a direct AxoBoard authorization URL and callback. The fresh Google client ID/secret and token-store encryption key still need to be placed in the protected direct-OAuth environment file.
- HubSpot direct OAuth intentionally returns `501` until the Google path passes end-to-end.
- HubSpot's current technology-partner guidance requires approval before public use of its sprocket. The mark is restricted to the internal beta until approval is recorded.
- The Murphy Door OAuth workflow intentionally records only an ephemeral handoff test. A real authorization cannot begin until fresh AxoBoard-owned provider apps and callbacks are registered.
- Display pairing, snapshot delivery, secure links, drilldowns, and automation actions are high-fidelity simulations; no device player, delivery worker, or rules engine exists yet.
- Automated mobile QA currently uses Chromium; physical Safari/iOS VoiceOver and Android TalkBack remain production release gates.
